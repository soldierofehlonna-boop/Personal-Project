"Interesting," he says, to nobody, in a language this place has never heard.

The word goes out and doesn't come back. No echo off the stone -- the vines eat it. Somewhere out in the scrub a bird carries on with whatever it was saying before.

He starts with the wood, because the wood is the thing that's been worked.

Eleven pieces, he counts, none longer than his forearm. He picks one up -- light, seasoned, not green -- and turns the cut end to the light. Clean. Two strokes, maybe three, biting from opposite sides and meeting slightly off-true in the middle. Not a saw. Something with a head on it, swung by somebody who does this often enough not to be careful about it. He sets the piece back exactly where it was, butt-out, the way it had been stacked.

The ring next. He squats -- badly, with a hand on the wall -- and looks at the ash instead of poking it.

It's layered. There's a grey, fine, settled bed underneath, and on top of that a coarser black scatter with recognisable stick-ends in it. Two fires at least. Maybe more; he can only really tell that the bottom of it has had time to go to powder and the top hasn't.

Under the lintels, the dry ground has a shape pressed into it. Not a bed. Nothing so committed. A patch about the size of a man sitting with his back to the wall, scuffed where heels would go, and beside it a smaller, rounder depression where something with a flat bottom sat and sat again.

One patch. One set of heel-scuffs. One place to put the pot down.

He does not find water. He looks -- properly, working the wall line in both directions, listening at the low end for the particular sound moving water makes -- and there's nothing, and the not-finding takes longer than the finding did.

So. One person, coming and going, who owns an axe and a pot and thinks this corner is worth keeping wood dry in. Not a camp. A stop. Somewhere on somebody's regular round.

That's when he sits down, because his ankle has been filing complaints for ten minutes and he's been ignoring them the way he used to ignore his back.

He gets himself against the back wall, on the dry ground but off to the side of the pressed patch -- some reflex about other people's chairs -- and gets the bad leg up on one of the fallen stones, boot and all. The relief is immediate and slightly obscene. He lets his head go back against the stone.

Sun's climbing. The dew's coming off the scrub in a thin haze where the light hits it. He has been in the world of Eldara for something under two hours and he has established: there is a man with an axe, there is a fire that isn't lit, there is water somewhere that isn't here, and his mouth is already dry enough that he's noticed it.

The knock-on from that is a list he's made a thousand times for other people's problems, and he runs it now without particularly meaning to. Water first. Then shelter, which he's sitting in and doesn't own. Then whoever's hearth that is downhill --

Tock.

Faint. Off to the northeast, past the ruin, somewhere in the trees.

Tock. Tock.

Steady. Spaced. The unhurried rhythm of a man who has a lot of wood to get through and all morning to do it in.
